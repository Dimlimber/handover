What buyers of trades businesses look for

Private-equity-backed groups, a competitor across town, a senior technician with a bank loan: they all check the same things. Here is what separates a business that attracts several offers from one that sits.

01

Service agreements and repeat revenue

Maintenance plans are the most valuable thing you can show. Know how many you have, what they bring in, and how many renew each year.

02

Technicians who will stay

A buyer wants to know how long your team has been with you, how they are paid, and whether they will stay.

03

A business that isn’t you

Whose license does the business operate under? Who quotes the big jobs, who do the commercial accounts call, and who dispatches?

04

Clean books, and job-level numbers

Three years of financials that tie to the tax returns, with documented add-backs, and margins by type of work: service, replacement and new construction.

05

Customer and equipment records

A customer list in a proper field-service system, with equipment age and service history, is an asset a buyer can market to. A box of paper invoices isn’t.

06

Fleet, equipment and premises

A list of vehicles with their age and condition, whether they are owned or financed, and whether the lease on the shop can pass to a buyer.

What you get back

We turn what you already have into the financials, buyer materials and document room you need before going to market.

  • Recast financials

    Your books were prepared for tax reporting, not for a sale. A buyer needs a clear view of what the business earns for its owner.

    Not an audit
  • Customer analysis

    Buyers worry that customers may leave with the owner. We use your sales records to show how durable those relationships are and where the risks sit.

  • Your story, written for buyers

    Buyers look at dozens of businesses. The memo is what makes them pick up the phone about yours. We write it from our conversation with you and from the numbers, in plain language, and you approve every word.

  • An organized document room

    Deals slow down, and sometimes die, while a seller hunts for paperwork. We collect, sort and index everything a buyer’s checks will ask for, and tell you what is missing while there is still time to find it.

  • A fix-it list

    We rank the issues by their likely effect on value and the chances of closing, then show what each one will take to fix.

  • An indicative value range

    Most owners have a number in mind. Few have evidence for it. We show you what businesses like yours have actually sold for, apply that to your recast earnings, and give you a range you can defend.

    Not a formal valuation
