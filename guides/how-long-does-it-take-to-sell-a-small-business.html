How long does it take to sell a small business?

From deciding to sell to money in the bank, a small business sale usually takes nine months to two years. Here is where the time goes, and which parts you can shorten.

By Dimitri, Handover · 20 September 2026

The guide

The short answer

Selling a small business typically takes six to twelve months from listing to closing, and longer for larger businesses. Add three to twelve months of preparation beforehand, and a handover period afterwards. Owners who plan to retire at a set date should start two to three years ahead.

The figures

  • Brokers surveyed for the IBBA and M&A Source Market Pulse report for the second quarter of 2026 said sales of smaller "Main Street" businesses took six to ten months from engaging a broker to closing. Businesses worth $2 million to $5 million took about eleven and a half months.
  • BizBuySell's Insight Report put the median time on the market at around 170 days for businesses sold in 2025, with wide differences between industries.

Both figures only count businesses that sold. Neither counts the preparation before listing or the handover after closing.

Where the time goes

Stage Typical time What happens
Preparation 3 to 12 months Records organised, financials recast, problems fixed
Choosing a broker and listing 1 to 2 months Valuation, listing agreement, marketing materials
Finding a buyer 3 to 6 months Enquiries, confidentiality agreements, meetings, offers
Due diligence 1 to 3 months The buyer and their accountant check everything
Financing and legal 1 to 3 months, overlapping Loan approval, purchase agreement, landlord consent
Closing A day Signatures and funds
Handover 1 to 12 months You stay on to train the new owner, as agreed in the deal

What slows a sale down

Slow answers in due diligence. A buyer asks for three years of statements and gets them in pieces over five weeks. Every delay gives them time to cool off, or find another business. Having the documents ready before you list is the single biggest time-saver.

The bank. A lender that cannot tie your statements to your tax returns will ask questions for weeks, then may say no. Clean, recast financials get approved faster.

The landlord. Consent to assign a lease can take a month or more. Ask early.

The price. An over-priced business sits. After a few months buyers assume something is wrong. Then the price drops, and it looks like a business in trouble.

Surprises. A licence that does not transfer, a customer contract that ends on a sale, a tax issue. Each one restarts a negotiation.

What you can shorten, and what you can't

You cannot do much about how long it takes for the right buyer to appear. You can do a great deal about everything after they do. A prepared seller can get through due diligence in four to six weeks. An unprepared one can take four months, and risks losing the buyer.

If you want to be out by a certain date, count backwards.

That allows up to a year to prepare, a year to sell, and a handover period. It feels early. It isn't. The owners with the best outcomes are the ones who were ready before they needed to be, because they could wait for the right buyer.

If you need to sell sooner

It can still be done. Concentrate on the things that are quick and that lenders insist on: three years of clean, reconciled financials, documented add-backs, and a complete document folder. Those take weeks, not months. Be honest in the listing about what a new owner will need to fix, and price accordingly.
