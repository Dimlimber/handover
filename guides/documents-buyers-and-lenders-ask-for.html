The documents buyers and lenders ask for when you sell a business

A complete list of what a buyer, their accountant and their bank will request, organised the way they ask for it, so you can have it ready before they do.

By Dimitri, Handover · 20 September 2026

The guide

The short answer

Buyers and lenders ask for three years of financial statements and tax returns, current-year figures, sales by customer, the lease, key contracts, licences, an equipment list, and employee details. Lenders on SBA-backed loans generally insist on three years of business tax returns. Having all of it in one organised folder before listing shortens a sale by weeks.

Why this list matters

After a buyer's offer is accepted, they get a period, usually 30 to 90 days, to check everything you have told them. This is due diligence. It is where most agreed deals that fail, fail.

The buyer sends a request list. How quickly and completely you answer tells them what kind of business they are buying. Documents that arrive the same day, named and organised, build confidence. Documents that arrive in pieces over a month do the opposite.

What a lender needs

Many small business purchases are financed with loans backed by the US Small Business Administration, the largest programme being the 7(a) loan. These are capped at $5 million, and buyers typically put in around 10% of the price themselves. Rules change, so check sba.gov for current terms.

From the seller, lenders generally want:

  • Three years of business federal tax returns
  • Three years of profit-and-loss statements and balance sheets
  • Figures for the current year, no more than a few months old
  • Accounts receivable and payable, aged
  • A schedule of business debts
  • Details of any customer that makes up a large share of revenue

If your statements and your tax returns don't agree, the lender will want to know why. Work that out first.

The full list

Financial

  • Business tax returns, three years
  • Profit-and-loss statements and balance sheets, three years, plus current year to date
  • A recast of each year showing seller's discretionary earnings, with a documented add-back schedule
  • Bank statements, three years
  • Monthly sales for three years
  • Accounts receivable and payable, aged
  • A list of debts, loans and equipment leases
  • Sales tax and payroll tax filings

Customers and sales

  • Sales by customer, three years
  • Sales by product or service line
  • Customer contracts and service agreements
  • Your pipeline, order book or backlog

Property and equipment

  • The lease, with every amendment, and the landlord's position on assigning it
  • If you own the building: the deed, and your plan to sell or lease it to the buyer
  • An equipment and vehicle list with age, condition and whether each is owned or financed
  • Stock count and how it is valued

People

  • Employees: role, start date, pay, benefits, full- or part-time
  • Employment, non-compete and confidentiality agreements
  • Contractors, and how they are classified
  • An organisation chart, however simple

Legal and compliance

  • Formation documents and ownership records
  • Licences, permits and certifications, with expiry dates and whether they transfer
  • Insurance policies and claims history
  • Supplier and distributor agreements
  • Any lawsuits, disputes or regulatory issues, past and present
  • Trademarks, domain names, software licences

Operations

  • Written procedures for the main jobs
  • Key suppliers and terms
  • Systems and software, and who has the passwords

How to organise it

Number the folders in the order above. Name every file the same way: the year, then what it is ("2025 Business tax return"). Keep a one-page index at the top that lists what is there and, just as usefully, what is missing and when it will be available.

Do not share any of it until a buyer has signed a confidentiality agreement, and leave customer names out of what you send early on. Your broker will manage who sees what, and when.

What to do about gaps

Every business has some. A missing document is rarely fatal. A missing document that the buyer discovers for themselves, in week six, often is. Find your own gaps first, fix what you can, and be ready to explain the rest.
